The Supreme Court has called for stronger technological and regulatory measures to detect and remove child sexual exploitation and abuse material from the internet. A bench of justices J. B. Parthivala and K. V. Chandran directed the creation of a comprehensive national database to identify and remove such content across social media platforms. The court expressed concern over the continued circulation of this material, despite its 2024 directions. Highlighting that technological safeguards alone are insufficient, the bench emphasized the need to equip children with awareness and safe internet practices. It called for digital safety and age-appropriate education to be included in the core curriculum. The Union Government has been directed through the Ministry of Electronics and Information technology and the Ministry of Law and Justice to frame necessary guidelines, standard operating procedures and IT rules amendments. The government must file an affidavit detailing its implementation progress by the next hearing on December 10th, 2026. The Supreme Court has declared that the right to access and use of the internet is a fundamental right guaranteed to every individual, including children under Articles 21 and 19.1a of the Constitution. A bench of Justice J.B. Parthivala and Justice K. Vinod Chandran observed that children, irrespective of their age, enjoy these constitutional protections. Reversing earlier judicial hesitation on whether Internet access itself is a fundamental right, the APIC Scott affirmed that protecting children from the online harms cannot be a ground to deny them digital access altogether. Donald Trump has waived tariffs and several sanctions provisions under the Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026, providing relief to India and China from the specified tariff measures targeting major purchasers of Russian oil and gas. In an order, President Trump cited the national interest of the United States for the decision. The move came a day before the deadline for implementing the tariff provisions of the legislation, which he signed on September 18. The law provided for additional duties of up to 100% on goods imported into the United States from the five largest purchasers of Russian origin crude oil and natural gas. The waivers will remain in effect until the President or the Secretary of State, in consultation with relevant officials, determines that further action is appropriate. The development comes ahead of the US Secretary of State Marco Rubio's scheduled visit to India. He is expected to hold talks with external affairs minister Dr. S. J. The move comes amid a reported diesel supply deal between Russia and the United States as diesel prices rise in the US ahead of the November midterm elections.
