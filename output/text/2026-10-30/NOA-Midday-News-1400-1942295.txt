The Indian Space Research Organisation ISRO will launch the NBS-03 Navigation Satellite aboard the GSLVF-18 rocket from the Satish Thavan Space Centre in Sriharikota, Andhra Pradesh at 11.51 tonight. Our correspondent reports that this milestone 20th GSLV flight aims to boost India's independent navigation system by deploying an advanced satellite equipped with an indigenous atomic clock. This mission marks the 20th flight of India's Geosynchronous Satellite Launch Vehicle GSLV. The rocket will deploy the satellite into a geosynchronous transfer orbit. NVS-03 is the third second generation navigation satellite designed to maintain and boost the continuity of the navigation with Indian constellation NAVIC system. The NAVIC system provides positioning, navigation, and timing services covering the Indian mainland up to 1500 km beyond its borders. A successful deployment tonight will restore NAVIC's basic operational requirements to four satellites laying the groundwork for upcoming NVS-04 and NVS-05 launches to fully stabilize India's independent navigation capabilities. The Central Water Commission CWC has issued a special flood advisory for West Bengal and Asam, following a reported glacial lake outburst flood at Shintapu lake in Bhutan, warning that water levels in Alipurdwar and Dhobri districts could rise suddenly as floodwaters moved downstream through the Sankosh river system. In its advisory issued yesterday, the CWC said the glacial lake outburst flood had been reported by the Indian Embassy in Bhutan and that a flash flood in the upper reaches of the Poonat Sanctu River, known as the Sankosh, in India could lead to a sudden rise in water levels.
