Located at an altitude of approximately 13,000 feet in Uruchal Pradesh, the Sehla tunnel represents a significant engineering achievement in the challenging terrain of the eastern Himalayas. Built at a cost of around 825 crore rupees, the project comprises two tunnels measuring approximately 1,003 m and 1,595 m in length. It has been designed to provide more reliable connectivity between Tejpur in Assam and Tawang in Urrachal Pradesh, particularly during periods of heavy snowfall and adverse weather conditions. The tunnel holds immense strategic significance as it helps overcome the challenges posed by the high altitude Sehla Pass. Thus, improved connectivity facilitates the movement of armored forces, military equipment and essential supplies thereby strengthening operational preparedness in this sensitive border region. Beyond its strategic importance, the project also carries significant benefits for local communities. More dependable road connectivity can improve access to healthcare, education, essential services and markets while providing a boost to tourism and other economic activities. The Sehla tunnel reflects India's continued emphasis on developing modern infrastructure in remote and border areas, ensuring that geographical challenges do not become barriers to progress. A vital link between security and development, the Sehla tunnel stands as a symbol of engineering excellence, improved connectivity, and the nation's commitment to strengthen its border regions.
