Audio to knowledge

From the airwaves to an answer

Archive bulletins and live audio go through one pipeline. Each stage keeps the metadata needed to trace an answer back to its broadcast, date, time and second of audio.

  1. 01

    NewsOnAIR

    Public source of All India Radio news bulletins and audio.

  2. 02

    Audio / news collection

    The archive scraper downloads bulletins and respects robots.txt. Live mode reads a stream through ffmpeg in overlapping 30 s windows.

  3. 03

    Transcription

    faster-whisper with voice-activity detection and word timestamps, so every sentence keeps its place in the audio.

  4. 04

    Genre classification

    Every segment is labelled (Politics, Economy, Health, Sports and more) by a zero-shot or fine-tuned classifier.

  5. 05

    Chunking

    Broadcasts are split into sentences, with repeated headlines merged. Articles are split by section, and nothing is truncated.

  6. 06

    Embeddings

    all-MiniLM-L6-v2 vectors in FAISS, plus an SQLite FTS5 keyword index over the same passages.

  7. 07

    RAG retrieval

    Date, time and genre filters, then hybrid dense + BM25 search with a relevance gate that drops unrelated passages.

  8. 08

    Contextual answers

    Gemini writes an answer sized to the question. Every claim is cited and links back to its source.

The data source

Powered by India's Public Broadcasting Network

NewsOnAIR is the digital news platform associated with Akashvani (All India Radio) and Prasar Bharati. It provides news and current-affairs content, including audio broadcasts, transcripts and regional-language news.

VaniStream (AIR) uses this source as the foundation for its news ingestion and knowledge retrieval pipeline. Articles are collected through the site's public WordPress API and audio bulletins from its public bulletin archive, slowly and one request at a time. Each article keeps a link to its original page, and each bulletin keeps its MP3, broadcast time and listen page.

The knowledge assistant

Ask the news. Get answers with receipts.

The assistant retrieves the most relevant broadcasts and full articles, then answers only from them. If the sources don't cover your question, it says so.

  • Every claim cited[S1]-style citations open the full article or the transcript at the exact sentence.
  • Timestamps and audioBroadcast sources carry their date, time and audio range, and you can play the cited seconds.
  • Depth that fits the questionShort facts get a sentence or two. "Why", "compare" and "summarise" questions get a structured 300-600 word answer.
  • Genre-aware searchAsk about "sports news" or limit the assistant to one genre in the newsroom.
